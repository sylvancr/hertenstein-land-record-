    What the records say, in short

    • A lakeside patchwork of hay meadow, an oak park of more than a hundred trees, small woods, garden and buildings, on ground polished by the last glaciation, inside the federal landscape inventory for Lake Lucerne.
    • Strongest: the oaks, an orchid recorded beside the house, and rich underwater plant beds in the bays the canton names as fish nurseries.
    • Weakest: meadows mapped as over-fertile, a walled shore, several invasive plants, and no public records of amphibians, mammals or bats.
    • The lake is changing fast: quagga mussel since 2024, and a summer surface of 26.3 °C in June 2026.

    What it means for the drawings

    • No mapped stream. Neither the federal nor the cantonal water maps show a stream, open or piped, on the land. The overland-flow map does show storm runoff across 292 and 505. Ask the architect what the "re-open stream" line is based on.
    • Farm zone. All five parcels are in agricultural zone 1, outside any building zone. The amphitheatre, pond, boardwalks and parking would each need a permit for building outside the zone.
    • Legal forest across 292. A band of about 1.3 ha is forest in law. Wood pasture and grazing there need the canton's forest service.
    • Crop rotation land. About 0.85 ha on the meadows of 505 and 289 counts towards the canton's protected crop rotation quota. Gardens and orchards keep it; ponds, paths and buildings would use it up.
    • A shore the canton wants back. Most of the 50 m shore sections are rated strongly impaired or unnatural, mainly from the wall. Removing the wall and making a wetland fits the canton's revitalisation planning, inside a keep-free water zone.
    • Protected buildings and a protected meadow. The school, dorms and chapel are rated worth protecting and the gym worth preserving; 289 sits in a landscape protection zone.
    • No federal inventory object on the land (amphibian sites, fens, dry meadows, reserves, corridors), and never soil-mapped.

    Tensions to settle early

    • Food and meadow. An edible landscape wants fertile, worked ground; species-rich meadow wants lean ground. Both fit if they are kept apart.
    • Path and quiet shore. Weggis wants a public path round the peninsula; the canton wants quiet at the pond. Part of the route may cross the holding.
    • Tidy and alive. Deadwood and long grass read as neglect to some guests. Mown paths and a few signs settle most of it.
    • Who decides. Stella runs programmes; the land is owned and run by the Hertenstein side. Changes to the ground need their agreement.
